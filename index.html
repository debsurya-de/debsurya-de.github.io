Inference on supposedly hard problems.

I am a PhD candidate in Applied Mathematics and Statistics at Johns Hopkins University. I study the statistical and computational limits of high-dimensional inference. My advisor is Professor Dmitriy (Tim) Kunisky.

Research

My research is in probability, high-dimensional statistics, machine learning, and theoretical computer science. I am particularly interested in problems where statistical estimation is possible in principle but may be computationally difficult.

I use low-degree polynomial methods, second-moment methods, random matrix theory, and combinatorial techniques. My recent work studies low-rank estimation under inhomogeneous noise.

  • Statistical-computational gaps
  • High-dimensional inference
  • Low-degree polynomial methods
  • Random matrices
